Smell gas? Do this first

  1. Get out. Leave the door open behind you if that costs no time.
  2. Touch nothing electrical. No switches, no phones inside, no flames.
  3. Call from outside. Enbridge's 24-hour line is 1-866-763-5427. Or call 911.

We repair after Enbridge has made it safe. We're not a replacement for that call.

Who is allowed to repair gas equipment in Ontario?

TSSA, Ontario's fuel safety regulator, says: "Only TSSA-registered Fuels Contractors can legally do fuels-related work in Ontario."

That covers gas piping, and it covers the appliances on it. A handyman can't legally do it, and neither can a friend with a wrench.

How does a gas repair go?

  1. You tell us what's happening. The smell, the sound, the notice, the appliance that won't light.
  2. We find the cause. It's the pipe, a fitting, a valve or the appliance itself.
  3. We quote the repair before we do the work.
  4. We fix it and replace what has failed.
  5. We pressure test the line. Gas doesn't go back on until the test passes.
  6. We check the appliance runs properly and tell you what we found.

Common questions

Is a gas smell an emergency?

Yes. Leave, then call Enbridge at 1-866-763-5427 or 911. Don't look for the leak yourself. The full steps are here.

What's a red tag?

It's the notice Enbridge leaves when it finds an unsafe appliance. The red tag page explains the A and B types and the deadline on each.

Do you fix propane equipment?

No. TSSA lists us under natural gas, so this site is natural gas only.

Can I fix a small gas leak myself?

No. Tightening a fitting can look harmless and still leave a leak, and the law reserves gas work for registered contractors. Leave it to us.
